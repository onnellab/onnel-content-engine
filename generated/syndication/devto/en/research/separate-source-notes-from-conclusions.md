# How to Separate Source Notes from Your Conclusions

## Question

How do you separate what a source says from what you conclude?

## Short Answer

Give every quotation or paraphrase a source and a precise locator. Write your interpretation separately, preserve the source’s limits, and record what needs checking before you strengthen a conclusion. Make the boundary between someone else’s statement and your reasoning visible.

A reading log records what you read. This workflow addresses a narrower problem: tracing each claim to its origin without treating your inference as something the source said. Provenance is the record of where a note came from; an inference is the conclusion you draw from it.

## Three fields to keep apart

Use separate fields for source quotations or paraphrases, your interpretation, and the next check. These can be columns or labeled lines in any note-taking system.

An interpretation can be useful without being established. Keep its status visible when you copy the note into an outline or share it.

## A fictional example

The memo, participants, and results below are invented to demonstrate the method. They are not research findings. Imagine reviewing a fictional pilot memo about two note templates.

| Source note and locator | My interpretation | Next check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quotation: “Four of six participants chose template B at the end of the session.” Memo A, v1, §2. | B may deserve further exploration with this group. This does not establish better learning. | What reasons did participants give? |
| Paraphrase: Participants worked individually in a quiet room. Memo A, v1, §1. | I cannot extend this observation to collaborative work. | Would the same question matter in a shared task? |
| Paraphrase: No delayed recall task was included. Memo A, v1, §3. | Later recall remains unanswered. | If recall is our question, what evidence would address it? |

“Template B improves learning” would go beyond these fictional notes. A preference, a setting, and an unmeasured outcome should not merge into one stronger claim.

## Recommended Workflow

1. **Capture the source and locator.** Record a title, author or organization when available, and a link or file identifier. Add the page, section, paragraph, or timestamp needed to find the passage. For changing material, record the version or access date. A title alone may not identify the passage you used.

2. **Mark quotation or paraphrase.** Copy quotations accurately and use quotation marks. Label a paraphrase and use your own words without quotation marks. Preserve qualifications such as “in this sample” and “may.” If wording is unclear, save the passage rather than silently resolving its ambiguity.

3. **Write your interpretation separately.** Start a field labeled “My interpretation.” State what you think follows and why. If you connect several sources, give each a locator. Do not attach one citation to a paragraph containing additional claims the source never made.

4. **Preserve context and define the next check.** Record relevant participants, setting, timeframe, definitions, and exclusions only when supplied by the source. Mark absent details as unknown. Turn a gap into a specific question rather than an assumption. Prioritize questions that affect your decision; not every gap needs a new investigation.

5. **Correct with a short trail.** Update the working note when you find a transcription error or revise an inference. Keep a dated line stating what changed, why, and which passage prompted it. Mark the old conclusion as superseded so it cannot be mistaken for your current position.

## Limits of the method

Clear provenance does not establish source accuracy or sound reasoning. You still need to assess relevance, methods, and conflicting material. Avoid copying unnecessary personal or confidential details into notes or shared drafts.

## Conclusion

Before reusing a note, check whose words it contains, where they came from, and which conclusions are yours.

## FAQ

### Must I quote every source?

No. Paraphrase when appropriate, retaining the locator and meaning. Quote when the exact wording matters.

### Can I combine several sources in one conclusion?

Yes. Identify which part each source supports and label the synthesis as yours. Keep disagreements visible.

### What if I cannot locate the original passage?

Mark the note as unverified. Do not present remembered wording as a quotation. Find the passage before relying on it.
