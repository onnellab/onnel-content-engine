Mark Uncertainty Clearly in Research Notes

A question mark beside a sentence is easy to add and difficult to interpret a month later. Did you lack a source, find conflicting accounts or make an inference? Recording the reason helps the next reading session start with a useful question rather than the same uncertainty.

Question

How can I record uncertainty without making a research note vague or falsely confident?

Short Answer

Mark what is unknown, disputed or provisional; explain why, link the evidence and write a specific next check. Keep earlier reasoning when updating the note. These labels describe the state of your investigation, not a measured probability.

What Do the Labels Mean?

Uncertainty in research notes is a gap between a claim and what the available evidence supports. The following labels are a practical editorial convention, not a formal research standard. They can overlap: a provisional interpretation may depend on an unknown date.

LabelMeaning in this noteUseful next check
UnknownNeeded information has not been established.Locate a source that directly addresses the missing detail.
DisputedSources appear to give incompatible accounts.Compare their wording, scope, dates and versions.
ProvisionalAn interpretation is being used while important checks remain.Identify evidence that would support or challenge it.

Recommended Workflow

  1. Isolate the claim. Write one checkable statement. Separate an event's date from your explanation of why it happened; they may need different evidence.
  2. Name the uncertainty and its reason. Write “unknown: no dated record found,” not simply “maybe.” A search that found nothing does not prove that no record exists.
  3. Attach the evidence you actually read. Include the source, relevant passage location and version or access date. If accounts differ, keep both. Check whether they concern different editions or periods before treating them as a contradiction.
  4. Write a bounded next action. Replace “research more” with “check the dated announcement for whether this is the launch date or the revision date.” Record what result would change the note.
  5. Update without hiding the path. Add the check date, new evidence and revised wording. Preserve the earlier reason in a short revision entry. If the question remains unresolved, say so.

References

Do not turn an impression into a confidence percentage. These labels do not validate a source or settle a disagreement. A deadline does not make a provisional claim established.

The University of Leeds note-making guide provides broader background on organizing notes and recording source details. The three-label convention here is this article's suggestion, not a classification from that guide.

FAQ

Does “unknown” mean false?

No. It means the detail has not been established in this investigation.

Are two different dates automatically a dispute?

No. They may refer to different events, versions or time zones. Compare context first.

Can I remove the label after finding one source?

Only revise the note to match what that source supports. Keep any remaining limitation explicit.

Conclusion

A useful uncertainty marker includes a reason and a next check. Make the unresolved part visible instead of hiding it behind confident wording.
