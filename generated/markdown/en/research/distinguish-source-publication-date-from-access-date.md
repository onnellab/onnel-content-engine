# Publication date or access date: what goes in your notes?

You open a web guide today and see last year's date at the bottom. Does that tell you when the author published the article, or only the year the site claims for its copyright? Record the label and its location before deciding what the date means.

## Question

Publication date or access date: what goes in your notes?

## Short Answer

Publication date is the day a source was made public. Access date is the day you actually consulted it. A labeled revision date tells you something different: when the current text was revised.

| Date field | What goes there |
| --- | --- |
| Publication | The date displayed for the specific article |
| Revision | A separately labeled update date |
| Access | The day you viewed the source |

## Recommended Workflow

1. Look near the title and byline for a date attached to this page. Copy the label as it appears and note where you saw it.
2. Record the date you read the page and save the URL. If the work has an edition or version number, save that separately too.
3. If the page shows only a revision date, preserve it as a revision. If the original publication date is absent, leave that field unknown rather than borrowing a date from the footer.
4. When you consult a changed page later, keep the earlier observation with the notes it supports. Record the new access date alongside the new version instead of overwriting the old record.

For an invented page published 2026-05-12 and read 2026-06-03, record both with their labels and save the URL. If only a copyright year appears in the site footer, leave the page’s publication date unknown. Keep the labels in your notes, then follow your required citation style when preparing the bibliography.

## Conclusion

Keep each date with its own label. A URL, a page-level date when available, and your own access date give a reviewer enough context to understand what you saw.

## FAQ

### What if two dates conflict?

Check whether one belongs to the site and the other to the article. If both appear to describe the article, retain both labels in your working notes and mark the ambiguity for review.

### Should I always print the access date in my bibliography?

No. Keep it in your notes and apply the required style at the end. Styles differ in when they use access dates, especially for undated or changing pages.
